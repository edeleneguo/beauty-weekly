Makeup Industry News Section 01

GLOBALGalderma Agrees to Buy ZO Skin Health for US$1.5BM&A▾
Galderma has agreed to acquire physician-channel skincare brand ZO Skin Health for US$1.5B, extending its dermatology-led portfolio beyond prescription into professional skincare.
The deal adds a doctor-dispensed skincare brand to Galderma's injectables and dermatological skincare franchise. ZO Skin Health, founded by the late Zein Obagi, is distributed mainly through dermatology and medical spa practices — a channel that has become the fastest-growing route for premium skincare in the US. The transaction is the second-largest dermatology-adjacent deal of 2026 after the Kimberly-Clark/Kenvue clearance. Terms beyond the headline price were not disclosed at announcement.
GLOBALEU Clears Kimberly-Clark's US$40B Kenvue TakeoverM&A▾
The European Commission approved Kimberly-Clark's US$40B acquisition of Kenvue with divestment commitments, clearing the last major antitrust hurdle for the combination.
The approval came with divestment undertakings designed to preserve competition in specific European personal-care categories. The combined group will pair Kenvue's consumer-health franchises with Kimberly-Clark's tissue and personal-care scale. For beauty suppliers and contract manufacturers, the clearance consolidates one of the largest buyers of packaging and formulation capacity in the mass channel. The parties have indicated integration planning will run through 2027.
GLOBALFairfax & Wittington Take Boots Private in US$8.9B DealM&A▾
Fairfax Financial Holdings and Wittington Investments agreed to acquire Boots for about US$8.9B including assumed debt, returning the UK health-and-beauty chain to private ownership.
The transaction ends a listed-company chapter that had constrained Boots' ability to invest in store format and own-brand beauty. Private ownership is expected to accelerate the No7 and Botanics own-brand push and the chain's beauty-hall refits. Boots operates roughly 1,900 stores across the UK and remains the single largest health-and-beauty retail footprint in the market. The deal will be watched as a read on how much room remains for own-brand beauty inside legacy drugstore banners.
USEstée Lauder Names Gaétane Baudry to Lead Bobbi BrownEVENT▾
Estée Lauder appointed Gaétane Baudry as Senior Vice President and Global General Manager of Bobbi Brown, filling a role vacant since the brand's leadership reshuffle earlier this year.
Baudry joins from within the Lauder portfolio and will be responsible for Bobbi Brown's global product, channel and creative direction. The appointment lands in the same week the company also saw its Chief Communications and Public Affairs Officer Meridith Webster depart after five years. Separately, Coty named Kara Langan as General Manager of US mass beauty, and make-up artist Isamaya Ffrench departed her eponymous brand, leaving its future uncertain. Bobbi Brown has been one of the slower-growing brands in Lauder's makeup division.
USBluemercury Relaunches Own Colour Brand Lune+AsterMARKET▾
Bluemercury is relaunching its in-house makeup brand Lune+Aster with a new visual identity and an expanded category range across its US store fleet.
The retailer, owned by Macy's, is using the relaunch to push private label higher up its assortment — a move that directly competes with the prestige brands it also sells. Lune+Aster returns with refreshed packaging, updated formulations and a broader shade architecture. Bluemercury also appointed Stacie Borteck Dattner as head of merchandising this period. Private-label beauty has been gaining shelf share in specialty retail as retailers chase margin under tariff pressure.
USe.l.f. Reinvests US$50M Tariff Refund into PricingMARKET▾
e.l.f. Cosmetics said it will reinvest a US$50M tariff refund into price and marketing rather than margin, positioning the brand to hold its value proposition through cost inflation.
The decision keeps e.l.f.'s entry price points intact at a moment when several mass competitors have taken price. Management framed the refund as a chance to buy share rather than recover profit. The company continues to run a social-first launch model: its rhode brand entered Sephora across 19 European markets on September 30, 2026. e.l.f. also launched a virtual beauty experience on the ZEPETO platform during the period.
CNShiseido Sues Shein for €2.6M Over Unauthorised SalesMARKET▾
Shiseido filed suit against Shein claiming approximately €2.6M over the unauthorised sale of its cosmetics, the latest brand-vs-platform enforcement action in the channel.
The filing targets third-party listings of Shiseido cosmetics on the fast-fashion marketplace, arguing trademark and distribution breach. It follows a run of similar actions by beauty groups against cross-border platforms in 2026. Separately, French cosmetics exporters reported customs delays into China during the period, adding friction for brands moving stock through the same trade lane. The case will be an early read on how far marketplaces can be held liable for third-party beauty listings.
CNDr.G Enters US via TikTok Shop, Skipping Sephora/UltaMARKET▾
L'Oréal-owned Korean dermocosmetics brand Dr.G launched on TikTok Shop US on October 5, entering the market through social commerce instead of specialty retail.
The route lets Dr.G build demand through creator content and shoppable video before committing to a Sephora or Ulta shelf. It mirrors a wider K-beauty pattern: CJ Olive Young is expanding K-beauty distribution through Sephora doors in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong, while other Korean brands are testing direct social commerce in the US. For L'Oréal, the launch tests whether a dermocosmetics label can scale in the US without traditional retail distribution.

Scoring Methodology

Heat scores do not pursue absolute heat — they serve relative ranking among peer products. Core principle: only signals from the specific product SKU itself may be counted; brand heat must never substitute for product heat.

Total Score Structure

MVP stage uses 50/50.

Sales Score Formula

Sales Signal = Base × Platform Authority × Evidence Confidence × Time Decay
Countable signals: Bestseller / ranking / monthly sales·GMV / Sold Out·Restock / Hot·Trending tags / multi-channel distribution / authoritative retail endorsement
Evidence Confidence: explicit screenshot ×1.0 · platform official tag ×0.8 · third-party article mention ×0.6 · manual judgment ×0.4

Buzz Score Formula

Buzz Signal = Base × Platform Authority × Buzz Type Weight × Time Decay
Buzz three-tier weight: Organic ×1.0 > Editorial ×0.75 > Paid/Seeded ×0.45
Organic: user posts/comments/UGC/Reddit · Editorial: Allure/Vogue/NYMag/Cosmopolitan/Byrdie/WhoWhatWear · Paid: brand BGC/PR seeding/creator collab

Time Decay

0-30d ×1.0 · 31-90d ×0.8 · 91-180d ×0.6 · 181-365d ×0.4 · >1yr ×0.2
Long-term bestsellers may retain higher weight with continued evidence.

Display Score Tiers

90-98 Viral · 85-89 Trending · 80-84 Strong Interest · 75-79 Rising · 70-74 Niche Signal · 65-69 Low Confidence
Internal raw_score drives true ranking, never tampered; display_score capped at 98, floored at 65.

Core rule: A product cannot score high simply because its brand is hot. Only SKU-level sales/ranking/editorial/user-discussion/social/restock evidence counts. Brand heat without product-level evidence → background info only, no heat added.

Cross-panel consistency: Heat rankings and new-product radar use the same scoring formula and reporting-cycle data. The same product must have identical scores across both panels. The two panels differ only in product selection scope (Heat = market-wide TOP10 / Radar = last-4-week new launches); calculation is identical.
